# Privacy Policy

**Ludo** (Android, package `com.crylo.ludo`)

Effective 11 October 2026

**Ludo collects no personal data, and nothing you do in it leaves your
phone.** The app has no account or sign-in, shows no ads, contains no analytics
or crash reporting, and does not connect to the internet.

## What the app stores, and where

To work, the app keeps a small amount of data **on your device only**, in the
app's private storage:

- **Profiles:** the names you type in for players (for example "Asha") and each
  one's count of games played and won.
- **Saved games:** where the tokens are in a Ludo or Snakes & Ladders game left
  unfinished, and who was sitting where.
- **Settings:** sound, emoji and taunts, the board picked last, and which way
  names face.

You choose the names. They don't have to be real names, and nobody but people
using your phone ever sees them. You can rename or delete a profile at any time
from **Profiles** on the setup screen. Uninstalling the app deletes all of this
data.

## What leaves your device

Nothing is sent to the developer or to anyone else. The app has no internet
access and requests **no Android permissions** at all: not the internet,
contacts, location, camera, microphone or storage.

The only time the app's data is copied anywhere is when **you** move to a new
phone with Android's device-to-device transfer. The profiles, saved games and
settings come along, so a family's records aren't lost to a phone upgrade. The
app opts out of Google's cloud backup, so its data is never uploaded to a
Google account.

## Children

Ludo is a family game, and children may play it. Because the app collects no
personal information from anyone, it collects none from children either. It
has no ads, no in-app purchases, no chat, and no links out of the app.

## Third parties

The app uses no third-party SDKs, so no third party receives any data through
it. Google Play, which installs and updates the app, has its own
[privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).

## Changes to this policy

If the app ever starts handling data differently, this policy will be updated
first, with a new effective date, and the change will be visible in this file's
history on GitHub.
